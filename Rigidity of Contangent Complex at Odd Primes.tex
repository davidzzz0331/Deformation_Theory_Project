\section{Introduction}
Quillen stated his conjectures for essentially finite-type maps of
Noetherian rings, using projective dimension; the flat-dimension formulation
extends to arbitrary Noetherian homomorphisms. Under the finite-type
hypotheses, the cotangent complex is pseudo-coherent, and finite flat
dimension and finite projective dimension agree. The second conjecture
adds the assumption $\textrm{fd}_A B<\infty$ and predicts that $A\to B$ is locally
complete intersection. Its conclusion is therefore the stronger bound
one, under an additional hypothesis on the ring map itself. 

This distinction is substantive. For a field $k$ and
$A=k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$, the augmentation $A\to k$ satisfies
\[
 L_{k/A}\simeq k[1]\oplus k[2],
 \qquad
 \Tor_i^A(k,k)\ne0\quad\text{for every }i\geq0.
\]
The cotangent calculation follows from transitivity for
$k\to A\to k$, and the Tor calculation from the periodic resolution with
differential given by multiplication by $\epsilon$. Thus the bound two
is sharp, and finite cotangent dimension does not imply finite flat
dimension of the ring map. Moreover, the finiteness hypothesis concerns
vanishing of $D_i(B/A;-)$ for all coefficient modules in sufficiently high
degrees; boundedness of the ordinary homology $H_i(L_{B/A})$ alone is a
different condition.

The complete-intersection conjecture was proved in characteristic zero
by Avramov and Halperin, and in general by Avramov
. Avramov's theorem applies to Noetherian
homomorphisms locally of finite flat dimension. Turner obtained a different
positive-characteristic proof using an algebraic analogue of Serre's
theorem \cite{Tur00}. More recently, Briggs and Iyengar proved a stronger
rigidity theorem: under the same local finite-flat-dimension hypothesis,
vanishing of a single functor $D_n(B/A;-)$, for $n\geq1$, already forces the
map to be locally complete intersection \cite[Theorem~A]{BI23}. These
results do not remove the flat-dimension hypothesis. For the bound-two
conjecture itself, earlier results include Avramov's theorem when either
ring is locally complete intersection \cite[Theorem~1.5]{Avr99}, and the
theorem of Avramov and Iyengar for algebra retracts, that is, surjections
$A\to B$ admitting a ring section $B\to A$
\cite[Theorem~I]{AI03}.

Turner's work supplies the homotopical strategy used here
\cite{Tur02,Tur03,Tur09}. One studies an augmented simplicial algebra over
a residue field and compares two consequences of its origin and its
Andr\'e--Quillen homology. Its ring-theoretic origin imposes nilpotence on
certain homotopy operations. A highest nonzero Andr\'e--Quillen class in
degree greater than two, on the other hand, can be detected by operations
that remain nonzero under arbitrarily long iteration. Turner established
the relevant characteristic-two detection results and proved the bound-two
conjecture for Noetherian maps in characteristic two with Cohen--Macaulay
source \cite{Tur09}. His detection argument uses relations among higher
divided squares. As he explained in \cite{Tur02}, the odd-primary
relations available there did not give the admissible rewriting required
by that proof. The issue was not the absence of odd-primary operations:
Bousfield had already constructed them and computed the homotopy of free
simplicial commutative algebras \cite{Bou}.

Two recent preprints also bear directly on this question. Sch\"urg
\cite{Sch26} proposes a proof in characteristic two without the
Cohen--Macaulay assumption. Liu \cite{Liu26} claims the bound in positive
residual characteristic, with statements extending beyond the
equal-characteristic setting considered here. His argument also uses
universal chain representatives and detection of operation towers in a
free algebra \cite[Lemma~3.7 and \S4]{Liu26}. The present manuscript develops the nilpotence argument
directly on the original local derived fiber using uniform syzygetic
Artin--Rees, and treats detection for connected algebras with a
finite-dimensional degree-one Andr\'e--Quillen group. We cite these recent
preprints for comparison; neither is an input to the proof below, and we
make no claim of priority for the shared constructions.



\section{Outline}
Fix a morphism of Noetherian $k$-algebras $R\to A$. Standard localization techniques reduces the conjecture to the case where we consider a surjection of Noetherian local $k$-algebras $(R,\mathfrak{m})\to (A, \mathfrak{n})$. In this case, the flat dimension of the cotangent complex can be detected by tensoring with the residue field
\begin{proposition}[Stack 15.79.2]
    Let $R$ be a ring. Let $a,b\in \Z$. Let $K^{\bullet}$ be a pseudo-coherent complex of $R$-modules. The following are equivalent: 
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $K^{\bullet}$ is perfect with Tor amplitude in $[a,b]$.
        \item For every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$, we have $\Tor^R_i(K^{\bullet};k(\mathfrak{m}))=0$ for $i\not \in [a,b]$
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
Thus, under perfectness assumption, it suffices to show $H_i^Q(A/R;k)=0$ for $i>2$. We will therefore suppress the coefficient $k$ in Quillen homology from now on, unless other coefficients are used.


To prove the conjecture, Turner's program utilizes many tools from the homotopy theory of simplicial commutative algebras. For a $k$-vector space $V$, let $K(V,n)$ be the simplicial $k$-vector space characterized by having homotopy group concentrated in degree $n$, with value $V$. 

\begin{theorem}
    Given $A\in s \textbf{CAlg}_k$, which is connected, there exists a sequence of cofibrations 
    \[A=A(0)\xrightarrow{j_0} A(1)\xrightarrow{j_1} A(2)\xrightarrow{j_2}...\]
    with the following properties
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item For each $n\geq 1$, $A(n)$ is $(n-1)$-connected.
        \item For $s\geq n$, 
         \[H_s^Q(A)\cong H_s^Q(A(n))\]
        \item There is a cofiber sequence 
        \[ \textrm{Sym}_k(K(H_n^Q(A),n))\to A(n)\to A(n+1) \]
    \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
In particular, for $A$ such that $H_i^Q(A)$ vanishes for $i>n$, we may identify 
\[A(n)\cong \textrm{Sym}_k(K(H_n^Q(A),n))\]
and obtain a comparison map 
\[\phi_n: A\to \textrm{Sym}_k(K(H_n^Q(A),n))\]
inducing isomorphism on $H_n^Q$. The work of Bousfield, Goerss and Lada identifies the homotopy groups of the right hand side, which is generated by compositions of homotopy operations.

\begin{theorem}[Bousfield, 8.11]
    Let $\{a\}_{a\in A}$ be $\mathbb{F}_p$-basis for $\pi_*X$. Then $\oplus_{s=0}^{\infty} \pi_t(\Sym^s X)$ is the non-unital associative graded-commutative $\mathbb{F}_p$-algebra with generators 
    \[\{\alpha_{I}(a) | a\in A, I \textrm{ admissible}, \textrm{ excess}(I)\leq |a| \}\]
\end{theorem}
In particular, one could find a composition of homotopy operations, of arbitrary length, acting non-trivially on the degree $n$-element in $\pi_*(\textrm{Sym}_k(K(H_n^Q(A),n)))$. Now the proof reduces to two parts:

\begin{theorem}
    Suppose $n>2$. There is non-zero element in $y\in \pi_* A$ such that $(\phi_n)_*(y)=a$, and one could find a composition of homotopy operations, of arbitrary length, acting non-trivially on $y$.
\end{theorem}
The proof of the theorem is comparing the Quillen spectral sequences using $\phi_n$.
\begin{theorem}
    There is a convergent spectral sequence
    \[E^1_{s,t}:= \pi_t(\Sym^s ( H^Q_*(A/R;k)) )\Rightarrow \pi_{s+t} X\]
\end{theorem}


This is the step where Turner had to restrict to characteristic $2$: he needed explicit Adem relations, which is still absent to this day, among operations to control the differentials. 


On the other hand, for $A= P\otimes_R k$, operations on $\pi_* A$ has the following nilpotence property
\begin{theorem}
  There exists some integer $N$ such that any composition of strictly suspended operation of length $>N$ on $\pi_* A$ is zero. 
\end{theorem}
This is where Turner needed the assumption that $A$ is Cohen-Macauley, so he could reduce to the Artinian case where the maximal ideal is nilpotent.




\section{\texorpdfstring{Homotopy Operations on Simplicia $\mathbb{F}_p$-Algebras }{} }

\begin{theorem}[Bousfield, 8.11]
    For an odd prime $p$, and a simplicial $\mathbb{F}_p$-algebra $B$, there exists two families of operations 
    \[v_t: \pi_n B\to \pi_{n+2t(p-1)}B \quad \quad 2\leq 2t\leq n \]
    \[\eta_t: \pi_n B\to \pi_{n+2t(p-1)+1}B \quad \quad 3\leq 2t+1\leq n \]
The operations have the following properties 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The operations satisfy
    \[v_t(a\cdot u)=a^p u\] 
    for $a\in \pi_0B$
    \item Except the top operation, all operations are additive. The top operation is the divided power. 
\end{enumerate}








\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}
    For $k\geq 0$, let $I=(\delta_1,s_1,...,\delta_k,s_k)$ be a $(2k)$-tuple of integers, where $\delta_i =0,1$ and $s_i\geq 1$. The index $I$ is called \textbf{admissible} if $s_{i}\geq ps_{i+1}+ \delta_i$ for $1\leq i\leq k-1$.  Its \textbf{excess} is defined to be 
    \[\textrm{excess}(I):=\begin{cases}
        0 & k=0 \\
        2s_1+\delta_1 & k=1 \\
        2s_1+\delta_1 - \sum^k_{i=2}(2(p-1)s_i+\delta_i) & k\geq 2
    \end{cases}
    \]
    
    
    
    
    Define 
    \[\alpha_{\delta_i,s_i}:=\begin{cases}
        v_{s_i} & \delta_i =0\\
        \eta_{s_i} & \delta_i=1
    \end{cases}\]
    and 
    \[\alpha_I:= \alpha_{\delta_1,s_i}\circ ...\circ \alpha_{\delta_k,s_k} \]

    \textcolor{red}{If out of range just zero}

\end{definition}


\begin{theorem}[Bousfield, 8.11]
    \label{generator}
    Let $\{a\}_{a\in A}$ be $\mathbb{F}_p$-basis for $\pi_*X$. Then $\oplus_{s=0}^{\infty} \pi_t(\Sym^s X)$ is the non-unital associative graded-commutative $\mathbb{F}_p$-algebra with generators 
    \[\{\alpha_{I}(a) | a\in A, I \textrm{ admissible}, \textrm{ excess}(I)\leq |a| \}\]
\end{theorem}

In particular, the generators provide an infinite tower of non-zero operations.
\begin{corollary}
    \label{tower}
    Let $V$ be a $k$-vector space, and let $u$ be the generating class in $\pi_n(\Sym_k K(V,n))$. For any $j>1$, the index $I_j:=(0,p^{j},0,p^{j-1},....,0,p,0,1)$ is admissible, and thus the operation $\theta_j:=\alpha_{I_j}$ is non-zero on $u$ for all $j$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{lemma}[Degree-Weight Bound]
    \label{degree_weight}
   Let $X$ be a simplicial $k$-module. 
   \begin{enumerate}
    \item Suppose $d=\textrm{dim } \pi_1(X)<\infty$. Then $\pi_t(\Sym^s X)$ is zero if $t<2s-d$.
    \item Suppose $X= K(V,n)$. Then $\pi_t(\Sym^s X)$ is zero if $t<2s+n-2$.
   \end{enumerate}
   
   
   
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    Let $y$ be a non-zero monomial in $\pi_*(\Sym_k X)$. By Theorem \ref{generator}, we can express $y$ as product of $k$ admissible monomial generators
    \[y=\alpha_{I_1}(x_1)...\alpha_{I_k}(x_k)\]
    where each $I_j$ is admissible. Let $I_j:=(\delta_1,s_1,...,\delta_n,s_n)$. Admissiblity provides a lower bound on degree in terms of the weight 
      \begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
        \textrm{deg}(\alpha_{I_i}(x_i)) & = \textrm{deg}(x_i)+2(p-1)\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}s_i \\
        &\geq \textrm{deg}(x_i)+2(p-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}p^i \\
        &=\textrm{deg}(x_i) + 2(p^{n}-1) \\
        &=\textrm{deg}(x_i)+ 2 \textrm{wt}(\alpha_I) -2 
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

Since degree and weight both add in the product, we get 
\[\textrm{deg}(y)\geq 2 \textrm{wt}(y) + \sum^k_{i=1} \textrm{deg}(x_i)-2 \]
If $X=K(V,n)$, then $|x_i|=n$ for all $i$, and part $(2)$ follows easily.


If $d=\textrm{dim}(H^Q_1(Q))<\infty$. We see only $|x_i|=1$ will contribute to a negative summand, and each basis vector can only appear once in the product for it to be non-zero by graded commutativity. Therefore
\[\textrm{deg}(y)>2 \textrm{wt}(y)-d\]
so part $(1)$ follows.




  
\end{proof}














 

\section{Detection in Quillen Spectral Sequence}
Let $A$ be an augmented simplicial $\mathbb{F}_p$-algebra. The Quillen spectral sequence reads 
\[E^1_{s,t}:= \pi_t(\Sym^s ( H^Q_*(A)) )\Rightarrow \pi_{s+t} X\]
Suppose $n$ is the top non-vanishing degree of $H^Q(A)$, and let $u\in H_n^Q(A)$ be the generating class. Then, the element $\theta_j$ defined in Corollary \ref{tower} is an element in bidegree $(s,t):=(p^j,n+2(p^j-1))$. We can analyze the permanence property of this cycle: 

\begin{proposition}
    The element $\theta_j$ can be represented by a cycle $a\in F^{s}N(P)_t$, and $da\in F^{2s}N(P)_{t-1}$.
\end{proposition}
In particular, the spectral sequence differential $d_r$ is trivial on the representing class $a$ for $r<s$, as the target $F^{2s}N(P)_{t-1}\subseteq F^{s+r+1}N(P)_{t-1}$ is trivial in the subquotient $\frac{F^{s+r}N(P)_{t-1}}{F^{s+r+1}N(P)_{t-1}}$. 

\begin{proposition}
    The Quillen spectral sequence of $\Sym K(H^Q_{n}(A),n)$ degenerates on $E^1$. 
\end{proposition}

For $r\geq s$, the target groups will be a subquotient of 
\[E^1_{s+r,2s+n-3}\]
By Lemma \ref{degree_weight}, we see the target groups automatically vanish once we choose $j$ large enough. 

On the other hand, the there are no incoming differentials before page $s$, by comparing the spectral sequence with that of $\Sym K(H^Q_{n}(A),n)$. There are no incoming differentials on or after page $s$ simply by degree reasons. 

\textbf{extension}: by comparing the spectral sequences, we see that there is no extension problems: the difference lands in $\pi_{n+2s-2} \Sym^{s+1}(K(H_n))$. This group is zero by part $(2)$ of Lemma \ref{degree_weight}. 


\section{Nilpotence of Operations on Local Derived Fiber }
Let $(R,\mathfrak{m})$ be Noetherian local ring, $M$ a finitely generated R-module. Choose a resolution of $M$ be finite free $R$-modules 
\[...\xrightarrow{d_2}F_1\xrightarrow{d_1}F_0\xrightarrow{d_0}M\to 0\]




\begin{theorem}[Uniform Artin–Rees for Syzygies]
\label{Uniform}
For a fixed ideal $J\subseteq R$, there is a uniform integer $h\geq 0$ such that for every $n\geq h$, and $i\geq 0$, one has 
    \[J^nF_i\cap \textrm{im}(d_{i+1})\subseteq J^{n-h}\textrm{im}(d_{i+1})\]
\end{theorem}




Take $J=\mathfrak{m}$, one has the following corollary
\begin{corollary}
\label{tor_reduction}
    There is a uniform integer $h\geq 0$, such that for every $q> h$ and $i> 0$, the reduction
    \[\Tor_i^R(M;R/\mathfrak{m}^q)\to \Tor_i^R(M;k) \]
    is zero.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
    Let $a\in F_i$ represent a cycle in $\Tor_i^R(M; R/\mathfrak{m}^q)$, i.e $da\in \mathfrak{m}^qF_i$. By Theorem \ref{Uniform}, $da$ is also contained in $\mathfrak{m}^{q-h} \textrm{im}(d)$. We can thus find some $b\in \mathfrak{m}^{q-h}F_i$ with $d(a-b)=0$. By exactness, there is some $a'\in F_{i+1}$ such that 
    \[da'=a-b\]
    reducing modulo $\mathfrak{m}$, the class $b$ vanishes, and thus $a$ is a boundary mod $\mathfrak{m}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Nilpotence on Local Derived Fiber]
    There is an integer $l$ such that all composition of additive operations of length greater than $l$ vanishes on $\pi_* (P\otimes_R k)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Let $a\in N(P)\otimes_R k$ be a cycle, so that $da\in \mathfrak{m}N(P)$.  Let $\alpha_I =v_{t_1}....v_{t_k}$ be a composition of a length $l$ operation of the form $v_t$. By Lemma \ref{chain_lift}, $\alpha_I(a)$ is represented by iterated chain lifts $V_{t_1}...V_{t_k}(a)\in \mathfrak{m}^{p^l}N(P)$. By Corollary \ref{tor_reduction}, taking $l>h$ will ensure the reduction mod $\mathfrak{m}$ is becomes a boundary, and therefore represents a trivial class in $\pi_*(P\otimes_R k)$.


\end{proof}





\section{Chain Model}
Since the spectral sequence is defined via filtration on chains, we need to have a chain representative of the operations, which a priori is defined via universal homotopy classes, in order to analyze how the operations interact with the differentials. We begin with a quick review of the operations.

Suppose we are looking for homotopy operations on $\pi_*B$ for some simplicial $k$-algebra $B$
\[\pi_n B\to \pi_{n+t}B\]
By representability and adjunction, these operations are represented by classes in $\pi_{n+t}(\Sym K(k,n))$. Let $a: K(k,n)\to B$ be a representative of some class $\tilde{a}\in \pi_n B$, and $u: K(k,n+t)\to\Sym(K(k,n))$ to be a representative of $\tilde{u}\in \pi_n B$. The evaluation $u(a)\in \pi_{n+t}B$ is given by the composition
\[K(k,n+t)\xrightarrow{u} \Sym(K(k,n))\xrightarrow{a} B\]
Under the Dold-Kan correspondence, operations can be understood more explicitly: they are classes in $H_{n+t}(N(\Sym K(k,n)))$, which can be represented by some chain map 
\[k[n+t]\to N(\Sym K(k,n))\]
which corresponds to an explicit degree $n+t$ cycle $z_{n+t}\in N(\Sym K(k,n))$. 

\subsection{Suspension on Chain Complexes}

Let $C$ be the chain complex concentrated in degree $i$. There is an exact sequence of chain complexes 
\[0\to C\to D\to C[1]\to 0\]
where $D:= \textrm{Cone}(\id_{C})$ is the cone, which is acyclic. Then the connecting morphism 
\[\partial: H_{j}(C[1])\to H_{j-1}(C) \]
is the suspension isomorphism. In the case $C=k[n-1]$ and under Dold-Kan, the exact sequence becomes
\[0\to K(k,n-1)\to K(D_n)\to K(k,n)\to 0\]
We shall refer to these as \textbf{additive suspension}. The non-additive case happens when we apply the reduced symmetric algebra functor: we have the commutative diagram of simplicial commutative non-unital algebras
\[\begin{tikzcd}
	{} & {\textrm{Sym}K(k,n-1)} & {\textrm{Sym}K(D_n)} & {\textrm{Coker}} \\
	&& {\textrm{Sym}K(k,n)}
	\arrow["{i_*}", from=1-2, to=1-3]
	\arrow[from=1-3, to=1-4]
	\arrow["q", from=1-3, to=2-3]
	\arrow[from=2-3, to=1-4]
\end{tikzcd}\]




Note that $\Sym K(D_n)$ is still acyclic. After normalization, a class $z\in N({\textrm{Sym}K(k,n-1)})$ still injects to a class $i_*(z)$, which is a boundary. Choose $\omega$ such that $d\omega=i_*(z)$, and define 
\[\sigma z=q(w)\in N({\textrm{Sym}K(k,n+1)})\]
It is clear that $q(w)$ is a cycle since $w$ is coming from the kernel, and the choice of $\omega$ does not affect the homology class of $\sigma {z}$. 

\begin{theorem}[Bousfield]
    \label{suspended}
    Suppose $z_{pn}$ is the normalized cycle representing the top divided power operation, coming from the universal generator in 
    \[v_n\in \pi_{pn}(\Sym^p K(k,n))\] 
    Then, $\sigma z_{pn}$ represents the additive operation from the generator
    \[\pi_{pn+1}(\Sym^p K(k,n+1))\] 
    The input degree and output degree are both raised by $1$, and the resulting operations is no longer the top operation. In other words, all the additive operations in $v_t$ family are all suspended from the top divided powers from lower input degree. 
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}[Chain Lift with Controlled Differential]
\label{chain_lift}
Let $B$ be a simplicial commutative $\mathbb{F}_p$ algebra. Let $a\in N(B)_{n}$ and $2j<n$. There exists a chain lift 
\[V_j^n(a)\in N(P)_{n+2j(p-1)}\]
satisfying 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item If $a$ is a cycle, then $V_j^n(a)$ represents $v_j(a)$. 
    \item If $P$ is an algebra over discrete commutative ring $R$, $J\subseteq R$ an ideal, and $da\in J^m(N(B))_{n-1}$
    \[dV_j^n(a)\in J^{pm}(N(B))_{(n-1)+2j(p-1)} \]
    \item If $P$ is augmented over $k$, with augmentation ideal $I$, and 
    $a\in N(I^s)_n$ with $da\in N(I^t)_{n-1}$ for some $t\geq s$, then 
    \[V_j^n(a)\in N(I^{ps})_{n+2j(p-1)}, \quad \quad dV_j^n(a)\in N(I^{pt})_{(n-1)+2j(p-1)}\]
\end{enumerate}















\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    Let $v$ be the generator in $n$, and $u$ be the generator in degree $n-1$ of $D_n$, with $dv=u$. There is a simplicial $k$-module map 
    \[D_n\to N(B)\]
    by $v\mapsto a$, $u\mapsto da$. Since $da=0$, we see the induced normalized algebra map $a_*$ factors through $q$


\[\begin{tikzcd}
	{N(\textrm{Sym}^pK(k,n-1))} & \\
	{N(\textrm{Sym}^pK(D_n))} & {N(B)} \\
	{N(\textrm{Sym}^pK(k,n))}
	\arrow["{{i_*}}", from=1-1, to=2-1]
	\arrow["{f_{u}}", from=1-1, to=2-2]
	\arrow["{f_{}}", from=2-1, to=2-2]
	\arrow["q", from=2-1, to=3-1]
	\arrow["{{f_v}}"', dashed, from=3-1, to=2-2]
\end{tikzcd}\]
Let $z^n_j\in N(\textrm{Sym}K(k,n))_{n+2j(p-1)}$ be the cycle representing the operation $v_j$. By Theorem \ref{suspended}, $z^n_j$ is suspended from some $z_j^{n-1}\in  N(\textrm{Sym}K(k,n-1))_{(n-1)+2j(p-1)}$. Choose $\omega_j^n$ such that $d(\omega_j^n)=z_j^{n-1}$, with $q(\omega_j^n)=z_j^n$, we define 
\[V_j^n(a):= f(\omega_j^n)\]
It is immediate from construction that $V_j^n(a)$ represents the class $v_j(a)$. For $(2)$, we have by construction
\[dV_j^n(a)=f(d\omega_j^n)=f_u(z_j^{n-1})\]
The map $f_u$ is induced by sending the degree $n-1$ generator $u$ to $da\in J^m((N(B)))_{n-1}$; the generator $z_j^{n-1}$ is of the form $ s_1(u)\otimes ... \otimes s_p(u) $, where $s_i(u)$ is some linear combination of the image of $u$ under iterated degeneracy maps. The image $d_u(z_j^n)$ is then 
\[d_u(z_j^n)=s_1(da)...s_p(d_a)\]
since the degeneracy maps are $R$-linear, and each $da\in J^mB$, it follows that $d_u(z_j^n)$ is in $J^{pm}\in B$. It is clear that normalization respects the ideal filtration. $(3)$ follows from exactly the same argument.




















\end{proof}

\section{Quillen Spectral sequence}
By the standard model structure on $s \textbf{CAlg}_R$, one may choose a cofibrant replacement $P\to A$ such that $P$ is semi-free, i.e every $P_n$ is a free $R$-algebra.  Let $X:= P\otimes_R k$. Denote by $I$ the augmentation ideal and $QX:=I/I^2$ the indecomposables. Standard computation gives
\[QX\cong \Omega_{P/R}\otimes_P k\]
And therefore we have $H_i^Q(A/R)\cong \pi_i(QX)$. This provides the interpretation that Quillen homology of an augmented simplicial $k$-algebra $X$ is the homotopy group of its indecomposables $QX$. we have a descending filtration of $X$ by 
\[X\supseteq I\supseteq I^2\supseteq I^3\supseteq ...\]
which leads to a spectral sequence 
\[E^1_{s,t}:= \pi_t(I^s/I^{s+1})\Rightarrow \pi_{s+t} X\]
A standard fact on the model structure on $s \textbf{CAlg}_R$ says one may choose $P$ to be semi-free, meaning each simplicial degree $P_n$ is a free $R$-algebra. It follows that 
\[I^s/I^{s+1}\cong \Sym_k^s(I/I^2) \]
Since a chain complex of vector spaces splits as direct sums of its homology, $I/I^2$ also splits as the direct sum of its homotopy groups under the Dold-Kan correspondence.
